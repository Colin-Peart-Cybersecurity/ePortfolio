Cybersecurity Skills & Competencies

Cybersecurity demands technical, analytical, and communication skills. Technical expertise builds defenses and responds to threats. Analytical skills uncover patterns and assess risk in complex data. Communication translates findings for non-technical stakeholders so they can act on them. Together, these skills turn raw security data into informed decisions that protect organizations.

Applying My Skills: Northstar Investigation

Phishing analysis, incident response documentation, and MITRE ATT&CK mapping together demonstrate the full range of the aforementioned competencies. Analyzing a phishing email requires threat detection, examination of headers and network indicators, and operating system knowledge, while Python and SQL help automate searches and query SIEM data for related activity. Documenting the incident response shows professional communication: technical writing, analytical reasoning, and collaboration produce clear records others can act on. Mapping observed behaviours to MITRE ATT&CK strengthens threat intelligence by linking tactics and techniques to known adversaries. These activiities also support risk management, as assessing impact, reviewing identity and access controls, and recommending governance improvements and security awareness training to reduce future exposure. Professional Development

In cybersecurity, technical mastery is only the starting point. Threats evolve constantly, so professionals must commit to continuous learning through certifications, hand-on practice, and close attention to emeging attack techniques. Equally important is ethical responsibility: handling sensitive data with integrity, respecting privacy, and acting in the public interest. Finally, clear communication turns techincal discoveries into decisions that others can trust and act on. Together, these commitments build credibility and resilience, helping professionals protect organizations and the people who depend on them.
