03 Project - MITRE ATT&CK Framework

Northstar Financial Services

Project Title: Adversary Tactics, Techniques and Threat Analysis

Case Study: Suspected Unauthorized Account Access Following Phishing

Prepared By: Junior Cybersecurity Analyst

Framework: MITRE ATT&CK Enterprise

1. Introduction and Project Objectives

This project applies the MITRE ATT&CK framework to the simulated phishing incident investigated at Northstar Financial Services. The objective is to identify relevant adversary tactics and techniques based on the available incident evidence.

The analysis builds upon the findings documented in Project 02, the Incident Response Report. It examines how the phishing activity may be represented within the MITRE ATT&CK framework and identifies opportunities to improve organizational security monitoring and defensive capabilities.

Project Objectives

  • Identify relevant adversary tactics and techniques associated with the phishing incident.
  • Map observed activities to appropriate MITRE ATT&CK technique identifiers.
  • Distinguish confirmed observations from potential adversary activities.
  • Identify opportunities for improved security detection.
  • Recommend defensive measures to reduce the risk of similar incidents.

2. Incident Overview

On November 06, 2026, Northstar Financial Services experienced a simulated phishing incident involving an employee in the Logistics Department.

The incident began when Joaquin Fernandes received a fraudulent invoice notification email containing an embedded link. The employee accessed the associated fraudulent login page and submitted corporate credentials.

Subsequently, an unfamiliar authentication attempt was identified in the simulated authentication records. The associated multifactor authentication challenge was unsuccessful. Northstar's Security Operations Center investigated the activity and initiated containment and recovery measures.

Key Incident Observations

  • A fraudulent invoice notification email was received.
  • The email contained a link to a fraudulent login page.
  • The employee submitted corporate credentials.
  • An unfamiliar authentication attempt was identified.
  • The associated MFA challenge was unsuccessful.
  • The SIEM generated a suspicious authentication alert.
  • The affected account was secured through containment and recovery activities.
Evidence Limitation: The available simulated records do not establish that an unauthorized party successfully authenticated or accessed Northstar's organizational resources. All activities described in this case study are fictional and developed for educational purposes.

3. Understanding the MITRE ATT&CK Framework

MITRE ATT&CK is a globally recognized knowledge base that documents adversary behaviours observed during cybersecurity incidents and intrusions.

The framework organizes adversary behaviour into tactics, techniques and sub-techniques. It provides cybersecurity professionals with a common structure for understanding, analyzing, detecting and mitigating potential threats.

Tactics

Tactics represent the adversary's tactical objectives or the reasons behind particular actions. They describe what an adversary is attempting to accomplish.

Techniques

Techniques describe how an adversary may attempt to achieve a particular objective. They provide a more detailed description of adversary behaviour.

Sub-techniques

Sub-techniques provide additional detail about specific methods used to carry out a technique.

For this investigation, the Enterprise ATT&CK framework provides the basis for analyzing the phishing and credential exposure activities documented in the Northstar incident.

Reference: MITRE ATT&CK Official Website

4. MITRE ATT&CK Technique Mapping

The following mapping identifies techniques that are relevant to the observed activities in the Northstar phishing incident. Each mapping is evaluated against the available simulated investigation evidence.

ATT&CK ID Tactic Technique Northstar Evidence and Assessment
T1566.002 Initial Access Spearphishing Link The employee received a fraudulent invoice email containing a link to a deceptive login page. The employee accessed the page. Strongly supported by the simulated evidence.
T1056.003 Credential Access / Collection Web Portal Capture The employee submitted corporate credentials through a fraudulent login page. This behaviour is consistent with credential capture through a deceptive web portal. However, actual credential collection and transmission by an adversary were not independently established. Conditional mapping.

4.1 T1566.002 - Spearphishing Link

Tactic: Initial Access

Technique ID: T1566.002

Technique: Spearphishing Link

This technique describes the use of phishing emails containing malicious links to encourage recipients to access websites controlled or manipulated by an adversary.

In the Northstar scenario, the employee received an invoice notification email containing an embedded link. The link directed the employee to a fraudulent login page that requested corporate authentication credentials.

The use of a deceptive business-related email and embedded link is consistent with the behaviour described by MITRE ATT&CK T1566.002.

Assessment: This is the strongest technique mapping identified in the available incident evidence.

MITRE Reference: target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"> T1566.002 - Spearphishing Link

4.2 T1056.003 - Web Portal Capture

Tactic: Credential Access / Collection

Technique ID: T1056.003

Technique: Web Portal Capture

This sub-technique describes adversary activity involving the capture of credentials submitted through a web portal or login page.

In the Northstar scenario, Joaquin Fernandes submitted corporate credentials through a fraudulent login page. This behaviour is consistent with the type of credential collection associated with deceptive web portals.

However, the available evidence confirms credential submission but does not independently establish that the fraudulent website successfully captured and transmitted the credentials to an adversary.

Consequently, this technique is included as a conditional mapping rather than a confirmed adversary activity. Additional evidence, such as simulated phishing-site records or credential collection logs, would be required to establish this activity more conclusively.

MITRE Reference: T1056.003 - Web Portal Capture

4.3 Valid Accounts - T1078

MITRE ATT&CK T1078 describes adversary abuse of legitimate account credentials to obtain or maintain access to organizational systems and resources.

Although the Northstar employee submitted corporate credentials through the fraudulent login page, the subsequent authentication attempt was unsuccessful at the MFA challenge stage.

The available evidence does not establish that an adversary successfully authenticated using the employee's credentials.

Therefore, T1078 is not included as a confirmed technique in this investigation. It remains a potential area for further investigation if additional evidence of successful authentication becomes available.

MITRE Reference: T1078 - Valid Accounts

5. Attack Sequence Analysis

The following sequence illustrates how the observed Northstar activities relate to the MITRE ATT&CK framework. It represents the documented incident progression rather than a confirmed successful intrusion.

Sequence Observed Activity ATT&CK Mapping Assessment
1 Fraudulent invoice notification email delivered. T1566.002 Phishing link observed.
2 Employee accesses the fraudulent login page. T1566.002 User interaction observed.
3 Employee submits corporate credentials. T1056.003 Credential capture mechanism not independently verified.
4 Unfamiliar authentication attempt identified. Further investigation required. MFA challenge unsuccessful.
5 SIEM generates an unusual authentication alert. Detection and monitoring activity. Security monitoring response.

Attack Sequence Assessment

The incident demonstrates how phishing can be used to deceive employees into disclosing sensitive authentication information.

The subsequent unfamiliar authentication attempt indicates that the exposed credentials created a potential account compromise risk. However, the unsuccessful MFA challenge prevented the investigation from establishing successful unauthorized access.

The incident therefore demonstrates a potential attack progression that was interrupted during the authentication stage.

Important: The sequence does not establish that the adversary completed all potential attack stages. No successful unauthorized account access, persistence, lateral movement or data exfiltration was confirmed in the available evidence.

6. Detection Opportunities

The Northstar incident highlights opportunities to strengthen security monitoring and improve the identification of phishing-related activity.

Email and Web Activity Monitoring

Northstar should improve monitoring of incoming emails containing suspicious links, particularly messages that imitate legitimate business communications such as invoice notifications.

Email security controls should correlate suspicious messages with subsequent employee browser activity where appropriate.

Authentication Monitoring

The organization should continue monitoring authentication records for unusual login attempts, unfamiliar source addresses, repeated authentication failures and unsuccessful MFA challenges.

SIEM Correlation

The SIEM should correlate relevant email security, web activity and identity authentication events to provide analysts with a clearer picture of potential phishing-related account compromise.

MITRE ATT&CK Detection Reference

MITRE ATT&CK provides a detection strategy for Spearphishing Links under detection strategy DET0107. It describes opportunities to correlate incoming emails containing suspicious links with subsequent user-driven browser activity.

Northstar could use this guidance to improve the correlation of email, web proxy and authentication security records.

Reference: DET0107 - Detection Strategy for Spearphishing Links

7. Mitigation and Security Recommendations

Based on the identified activities, the following recommendations are proposed to strengthen Northstar's defensive security capabilities.

Security Area Recommended Action Expected Security Benefit
Email Security Strengthen email filtering, malicious link detection and sender authentication controls. Reduce exposure to fraudulent emails.
Employee Awareness Provide regular phishing awareness training and simulated phishing exercises. Improve employee recognition of social engineering attempts.
Identity Protection Evaluate phishing-resistant multifactor authentication. Strengthen protection against credential misuse.
Security Monitoring Improve SIEM correlation of suspicious email, browser and authentication events. Improve detection and investigation capabilities.
Web Security Apply appropriate web filtering and block confirmed malicious domains. Reduce access to known phishing websites.
Incident Response Maintain documented procedures for reporting phishing and responding to suspected account compromise. Improve response coordination and containment.

Recommended MITRE ATT&CK Mitigations

The following MITRE ATT&CK mitigation references are particularly relevant to the phishing activity identified in this investigation:

  • M1017 - User Training: Educate employees about phishing and social engineering techniques.
  • M1021 - Restrict Web-Based Content: Restrict access to websites that present unnecessary security risks.
  • M1054 - Software Configuration: Apply appropriate email authentication and security configuration controls.

Reference: MITRE ATT&CK T1566.002 - Mitigations

8. Conclusion

The MITRE ATT&CK analysis of the Northstar Financial Services phishing incident demonstrates how a framework-based approach can help cybersecurity professionals interpret and classify observed adversary behaviour.

The strongest technique mapping identified was T1566.002 - Spearphishing Link, which corresponds to the fraudulent invoice notification email and embedded link used to direct the employee to a deceptive login page.

T1056.003 - Web Portal Capture was identified as a conditional mapping because the employee submitted credentials through the fraudulent page, although successful credential collection by an adversary was not independently established.

The investigation did not confirm successful unauthorized authentication. Consequently, the analysis does not classify T1078 - Valid Accounts as a confirmed adversary technique.

The findings highlight the importance of layered security controls, employee awareness, effective multifactor authentication and continuous security monitoring.

Applying MITRE ATT&CK has provided a structured approach to understanding the incident and identifying opportunities for Northstar to strengthen its cybersecurity posture.
